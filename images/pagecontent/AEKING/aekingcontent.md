# AEKING
## A fake platofomr for IKEA

During my Bchelor thesis I explored the environmental consequences of mass-produced "Fast Furniture," focusing specifically on the global giant IKEA. While IKEA successfully champions democratic design by providing affordable goods, its reliance on fragile, hard-to-repair materials like particleboard creates a problematic cycle of consumption and waste. To combat this throwaway culture, this thesis proposes a systemic, sustainable, and economically competitive framework that uses the concept of "hacking" to extend the lifecycle of these ubiquitous products.

Hacking IKEA products is already a diffused practice, sponsered even by the company itself as these examples testimony:

At the center of this vision is AEKING, a conceptual web platform, to help users preserve, repair, and re-valorize their furniture. Designed with a bold, neo-brutalist aesthetic that subverts IKEA's traditional branding, the platform fosters a circular economy through community engagement. A core feature is an interactive map that connects everyday consumers with local "Hackers," skilled artisans, and FabLabs ready to assist with repairs or upgrades. Additionally, AEKING provides step-by-step "cure" guides for standard damages, downloadable 3D models for furniture modifications, and a dedicated marketplace to buy, sell, or donate upcycled pieces.
